What Is KultureCity? Inside Samantha Judge's Board Service

📅 September 24, 2026
✎ Editorial Team
⌚ 3 min read

Samantha Judge serves on the Board of Directors of KultureCity, a nonprofit focused on making everyday public spaces — stadiums, arenas, concert halls, museums, and more — accessible to people with sensory needs and invisible disabilities. Understanding the organization helps explain the shape of her public work.

KultureCity’s premise is straightforward: many people stay home from games, concerts, and outings not because they lack interest, but because the environment is overwhelming. For Sam Judge, a New York–based kinesiology researcher and marathoner, board service places her inside the governance of an organization working to change that.

How KultureCity Works

The organization partners with venues to make them sensory inclusive. Staff receive training on recognizing and responding to guests with sensory needs, and venues offer sensory bags containing items such as noise-canceling headphones and fidget tools. Many partner venues also designate quiet areas where guests can step away from crowd noise.

The result is a set of practical, repeatable measures rather than one-off gestures. A family attending an event at a participating venue can expect a baseline of support, which can be the difference between going and staying home.

What Board Service Involves

A board seat is an ongoing responsibility. Directors help set an organization’s priorities, oversee its direction, and represent it publicly. For Samantha, that has meant sustained involvement rather than a single donation or appearance, including taking the stage at KultureCity’s Hero Kulture New York event in 2024.

The most tangible outcome connected to that involvement is in Manhattan: the permanent MSG sensory room, which opened in 2024 and bears her name alongside her husband’s. It is built into a venue that draws visitors from around the world.

Fundraising on the Run

Judge has also supported KultureCity through endurance running. She finished the 2023 TCS New York City Marathon in 3:31:44 while representing the organization and ran the 2025 race in 3:32:34, again in support of its mission. The story of her marathons for sensory inclusion is told in a separate feature.

Her board role draws on a background that begins well before New York. Samantha’s academic and personal history, from Linden, California to graduate research at Fresno State, provides context for the evidence-based perspective she brings to accessibility work. She can also be followed through her Instagram account.

Conclusion

KultureCity’s work is practical by design: training, tools, and quiet spaces that let more people attend public events. Samantha Judge’s board service ties her to that work at the organizational level, and the sensory room at Madison Square Garden shows what that commitment can produce in a single, highly visible venue.
