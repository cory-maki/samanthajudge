How Samantha Judge's Marathon Journey Became a Platform for Sensory Inclusion

📅 October 1, 2026
✎ Editorial Team
⌚ 3 min read

For Samantha Judge, the marathon has become more than a personal test. Across multiple races, the New York–based KultureCity board member has used 26.2 miles to raise awareness and support for sensory inclusion — and, at the 2026 Boston Marathon, to help another athlete reach the finish.

Her running connects two parts of her background. Sam Judge studied human movement as a graduate researcher in kinesiology, and marathon training puts that subject into practice under real conditions: pacing, fatigue, and the cumulative load of months of preparation.

New York City, 2023 and 2025

Samantha finished the 2023 TCS New York City Marathon in 3:31:44 while representing KultureCity. The race runs through all five boroughs before finishing in Central Park, and running it for a cause put the organization’s name in front of one of the largest marathon audiences in the world.

She returned in 2025 and finished in 3:32:34, again running in support of KultureCity. The two finishing times, recorded two years apart, sit within a minute of each other, which points to sustained training rather than a one-time effort.

Two finishing times, recorded two years apart, within a minute of each other.

Boston 2026: Running as a Guide

At the 2026 Boston Marathon, Judge took on a different role. She ran as a guide for Adrienne Bunn, an athlete with autism, accompanying her over the point-to-point course from Hopkinton to Boston. A guide’s job is not to chase a personal record but to support another runner’s race through pacing, navigation, and steady presence over 26.2 miles.

The shift from racing to guiding mirrors the mission of KultureCity, which focuses on removing barriers so people with sensory needs can take part fully in public life. On the Boston course, that principle took the form of one runner helping another finish. The organization’s approach is outlined in a profile of KultureCity’s programs.

From Research to the Road

The running also connects back to her academic work. Her graduate research at Fresno State examined UCL injury risk in Major League pitchers, a project recognized as the university’s Outstanding Master’s Degree Project in 2017. Understanding how the body handles repeated stress is directly relevant to preparing for a marathon.

Running links, too, to the most permanent result of her advocacy: the Aaron and Samantha Judge Sensory Room at Madison Square Garden, opened in 2024. Samantha’s background and board work are summarized on her biography page, and readers can also find Sam Judge on YouTube.

Conclusion

Samantha Judge’s marathons trace a clear progression: racing to raise support for KultureCity, then running alongside an athlete with autism to help her finish. Each race reflects the same priority behind her board service and the MSG sensory room — making shared public experiences accessible to more people.
